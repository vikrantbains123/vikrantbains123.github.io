01About

I've spent my career building software and the teams behind it. Today I lead mobile engineering and work across functions on AI initiatives, where my job is to separate what's impressive from what's valuable.

Much of that work comes down to judgment. When is an open-weight model good enough? When is a frontier model worth what it costs? How do you bring a team along without slowing delivery? I try to answer these questions with evidence rather than hype.

I also stay close to the code. I build my own tools, run my own benchmarks, and treat weekend experiments as a way to keep my opinions honest.

02Perspective

Measure before you scale.

An AI initiative earns more investment when it shows gains in cost, speed, or revenue. A good demo isn't enough.

Right-size the model to the task.

Most work doesn't need the largest model. Routing by task complexity across open and frontier models is one of the most reliable ways to control AI spend at scale.

AI literacy is a leadership responsibility.

Teams adopt what they understand. I favor small, practical habits that build that understanding over mandates and big programs.

03Philosophy

  • People first, then process, then tools.

    Great teams build great systems. I invest in the people before I optimize anything else.

  • Clarity is a form of respect.

    Clear goals, honest feedback, and explicit expectations let people do their best work without guessing.

  • Outcomes over output.

    Shipping more doesn't mean much on its own. What matters is moving the metrics that matter to customers and the business.

  • Grow people beyond their current role.

    My job is to make my engineers ready for the next level, and to make sure they get the credit when they reach it.

  • Ship small, learn fast.

    Short feedback loops beat long plans. Iterate in the open and let real usage guide the roadmap.

  • Lead close to the work.

    I stay hands-on enough to ask the right questions, earn credibility, and spot problems early.

  • Learn and be curious.

    I keep learning by building tools, benchmarking new models, and exploring ideas outside my comfort zone.

  • Bias for action.

    Speed matters. Most decisions are reversible, so I make the call, take calculated risks, and adjust as we learn.
